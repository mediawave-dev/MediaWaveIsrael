# 02 Awwwards: how the jury scores, what studio winners do, what to steal

Research date and access date for every source: 2026-10-16. Sources are numbered [S#] at the end; every claim cites one. UNVERIFIED marks anything I could not confirm at a primary source.

## Executive summary

1. The jury weights are Design 40, Usability 30, Creativity 20, Content 10; at least 18 jurors, the 3 scores furthest from the average are dropped, HM needs 6.5+ [S1][S2].
2. The Developer Award goes to SOTD winners scoring above 7 with the dev jury [S1]; a SOTD with 6.79 got no Dev Award [S22].
3. Back-calculated from four published score cards, the dev weights are WPO 0.20, Responsive 0.20, Semantics/SEO 0.20, Markup 0.15, Animations 0.15, Accessibility 0.10 [S5][S10][S11][S16].
4. In 22 winner score cards I read, Usability was the lowest jury category 21 times, and Accessibility the lowest (or tied lowest) dev category 16 times, never above 7.4.
5. So the cheapest place to beat other winners is not more spectacle: it is usability and accessibility on a phone.
6. A sitting juror says he scores on a laptop and a mid-range phone, desktop-only caps at HM, a 2 s branded reveal is craft and a 6 s progress bar is a penalty [S6].
7. The closest analogues to the MediaWave one-shot are San Rita (one continuous camera over a paper-grain map that is also the navigation, SOTD Mar 2026) and Bruno Simon (one persistent object in one world, SOTM Jan 2026) [S11][S10].
8. Studio winners that sell well show a plain H1, a visible contact path with two routes, and real work as proof (Lama Lama, twks, Clou) [S14][S17][S18].
9. Inner pages are judged as part of the same world: 404s, contact pages and page transitions are listed as featured elements on winners [S17][S19][S16].
10. Top methods for MediaWave: the camera move as the route change, a reduced-motion final-frame version, a visible chapter rail, paper behaving like paper under the finger, and the contact submit as the finale.

## Q1. Jury criteria, thresholds, penalties, mobile

**Scoring.** Design 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20%, Content 10%; minimum 18 jurors; the 3 scores furthest from the average are eliminated; voting lasts 5 days and a site can win SOTD early with a high jury score plus 10 PRO votes [S1]. A site has up to 3 months after approval to win SOTD; jury scores are only shown for SOTD winners [S2]. The overall SOTD score is exactly the weighted mean (Bruno Simon: 8.05/7.83/8.62/8.18 gives 8.11) [S10].

**Thresholds.**
- Honorable Mention: jury 6.5 or more [S1]; the FAQ adds that the user score must also reach 6.5 [S2].
- Site of the Day: highest score of the day, no fixed number [S1]. Observed SOTD scores in this research: 7.22 (Realevate) to 8.11 (Bruno Simon) [S22][S10].
- Developer Award: SOTD winners reviewed by the developer jury, awarded above 7 [S1]. Simonholm.studio won SOTD with dev 6.79 and is listed without the Dev Award [S22][S3].
- Site of the Month: the 8 highest-scoring sites of the month are re-reviewed and user votes carry extra weight [S1]. Lama Lama became SOTM at 7.51 (users 8.10) while higher SOTDs did not, so community votes matter [S14].
- Site of the Year: all SOTMs nominated, announced in February [S1].

**Developer categories.** Semantics/SEO, Animations/Transitions, Accessibility, WPO, Responsive Design, Markup/Meta-data [S5]. The official Developer Guidelines were not fetchable (UNVERIFIED), but the weights above reproduce four published dev totals to two decimals. WPO, Semantics and Markup carry 0.55 of the dev score.

**What gets penalized.** The official evaluation page does not mention mobile, performance or accessibility at all [S1]. Juror Hon Tran lists jank (a WebGL hero at 20 fps), broken mobile layouts, effects without hierarchy, missing focus states, no reduced-motion path, low contrast and custom cursors that hide the real one [S6]. Usability is described as covering navigation, clarity, performance, responsiveness and accessibility [S7]. A studio guide warns that "inner pages feel like a different site" is a common failing and that judges check mobile first [S8].

**How mobile is judged.** 2026 SOTD pages show no separate mobile score, only a featured "mobile thumbnail" beside the desktop one [S17][S19][S15]. Mobile enters through Usability (30%) and Responsive Design (0.20 of dev). Hon Tran: "I score on real devices", laptop plus mid-range phone [S6]. The Mobile Excellence badge launched in 2017 at 75/100, judged by a Google-backed panel [S9]; whether it still runs in 2026, and the 70/100 figure in [S7], are UNVERIFIED.

## Q3. Signature moments, and which survive a phone

Counted from featured elements on the 22 score cards read [S10] to [S22]:

- **Intro or loader** (8): Lama Lama, San Rita (loader with steps such as "Terraforming activated"), Phantom, twks, KVS, Elliott Mangham, Realevate, Obys. Rule from a juror: about 2 s and branded, never a long progress bar [S6]. Phone: yes, if it is the first frame of the page rather than a gate.
- **Page transitions** (6): Jesper, Obys, San Rita, KVS, Realevate, Immersive Garden (menu). Phone: yes, they are tap-driven.
- **Contact or form moment** (4): Lama Lama step-inside, Studio Dialect live time, twks forms, Why Zero's "Waitlist Form with Personalized Origami" (SOTM Sep 2026) [S22]. Phone: yes, the most transferable.
- **Footer finale** (4): Phantom, Studio Dialect, San Rita, Form&Fun. Phone: yes.
- **404** (3): twks, REF Digital, Boc. Phone: yes.
- **Sound** (3): KVS, Why Zero, Bruno (music, toggle). Phone: only opt-in.
- **Cursor and hover** (3): Studio Dialect, Form&Fun, REF. Phone: no; Utsubo says touch must replace hover on purpose [S8].
- **Touch-native gestures:** Why Zero's "Hold to Proceed" and Bruno's one-finger/two-finger map [S22][S10].

A studio guide recommends one signature moment rather than many effects [S8]; the Immersive Garden and San Rita teams both credit cutting features [S13][S11].

## Q4. Inner pages as one world

- **Services:** Lama Lama gives each offer its own page under the same header and CTA pair [S14]; twks lists Branding, Campaign, Digital [S17].
- **Case studies:** reached by a continuous move, not a hard cut: San Rita's camera dive with a persistent canvas [S11], Jesper's project-to-project transition [S16]. Immersive Garden adds a Backstage layer of process [S13]. Obys offers three views of the same list [S15].
- **Legal:** plain and readable: Clou's Impressum and AGB PDF, Phantom's privacy link [S18][S20]. No winner in this set made legal theatrical.
- **404:** featured on twks, REF Digital and Boc; twks, Lama Lama, Boc, REF and Jesper all return a true 404 status (curl, 2026-10-16) [S17][S19][S22].
- **Failure to avoid:** "inner pages feel like a different site" [S8].

## Q5. Failure modes, with evidence

- **Scroll-jacking.** NN/g usability tests: "scrolljacking leads to more user pain than benefits"; worse on mobile; users thought pages were broken or finished; advice: justify it, keep direction consistent, keep normal-scroll sections, keep key text out of it [S23].
- **Mystery navigation.** NN/g (179 users): hidden nav used 27% on desktop vs 48% visible; on mobile hidden 57% vs partly visible 86%; tasks 15% slower on mobile with hidden nav [S24]. Bruno's game navigation drew Usability 4 and 5 from two jurors [S10].
- **Loaders.** "A six-second progress bar is a usability penalty wearing a costume" [S6].
- **Accessibility.** Lowest dev category on 16 of 22 winners here. ZURB Radar: Lacoste Polo Factory scored 9.20 on animations and 6.00 on accessibility [S26]. WebAIM Million 2026: 95.9% of home pages fail; low contrast on 83.9% [S25]. WCAG 2.3.3 (AAA) asks that interaction-triggered motion can be disabled, via prefers-reduced-motion [S27].
- **No contact path.** Phantom's and San Rita's home text showed no email or form; Bruno's only route is Discord [S20][S11][S10]. Lama Lama and twks show the opposite: phone, email, two CTAs [S14][S17].
- **Usability in general.** Usability was the lowest jury category on 21 of 22 winners here. A University of Warsaw eye-tracking study frames award sites as often criticized for low usability (findings not published on the page: UNVERIFIED) [S28].

## Methods to apply to the MediaWave one-shot site (ranked)

1. **The camera move is the route change** (San Rita, Jesper). Applies: choose/services into the six service pages, and the portfolio page. Tapping a service sheet makes the camera settle onto that sheet, which becomes the page; the desk layer stays mounted. It extends the one shot across routes with no new look, and a tap is native on a phone.
2. **A reduced-motion "final frame" version, all text in the DOM** (16 of 22 winners lowest on accessibility). Applies: every chapter, especially the laptop build and the pull-back finale. Each scene jumps to its landed state; text on the laptop screen and the final phone is real HTML. Ink on white already passes contrast, so this is the cheapest lead over other winners.
3. **A visible chapter rail plus an always-visible contact button** (NN/g on hidden nav and scrolljacking). Applies: the global header over home. Five ink tick marks for meet, choose, build, start, finale, tappable, with one persistent contact button. It cures the "illusion of completeness" inside one long camera move.
4. **Paper behaves like paper under the finger** (Mr. Pandas, San Rita grain). Applies: every sheet landing and every card. Press sinks the sheet and tightens its shadow; release lifts a corner; landings slide a few pixels on air. It is grey/white/ink only, and touch-first by design.
5. **The contact submit is the finale's signature** (Lama Lama step-inside, Why Zero origami form, Studio Dialect live clock). Applies: start/FAQ+contact and the pull-back finale. The filled sheet folds and slides under the phone that the camera then pulls back to; nearby, an honest line with the desk's local time. Two routes as at Lama Lama: WhatsApp now, or the form with the six services as first question.
6. **The back of every sheet is the proof** (Immersive Garden Backstage). Applies: the portfolio page and service pages. Tap flips a sheet: what the client asked, what was built, the live link. Two-sided paper is native to the concept and adds content without new screens.
7. **A 2 s intro that is the first frame of the shot** (Obys, Lama Lama, juror rule). Applies: home hero. The first sheet lands on the table; no percentage, no gate, content already in prerendered HTML.
8. **Chapter numbers printed on the sheets** (Immersive Garden numerals as menu anchors). Applies: home chapters and the menu. 01 to 05 set in the heading style on each sheet, the same numbers in the rail and the menu.
9. **Server-rendered semantics everywhere** (Obys 8.0/8.0, twks schema on its 404). Applies: all pages. Keep prerendering, add ProfessionalService/LocalBusiness data on every route; WPO, Semantics and Markup are 0.55 of the dev score.
10. **Two densities of the portfolio** (Obys three views, Jesper Featured/Full). Applies: portfolio page. "On the desk" sheets and a plain index list; the list is the phone default.
11. **A 404 that is an empty spot on the same desk** (twks, REF, Boc). Applies: 404. A sheet-shaped gap on the grey table and a link home, with a true 404 status.
12. **Legal pages as one plain sheet** (Clou, Phantom). Applies: legal pages. Same header and type, no camera move, comfortable reading width.
13. **An adaptive quality ladder** (San Rita, Bruno's quality toggle). Applies: home and finale. On weak phones drop shadows and grain first; never drop the camera move or the text.
14. **A restraint ledger** (San Rita cut features, Immersive Garden removed more than it added, Obys cut its archive). Applies: every chapter. One signature per chapter; everything else is logged as cut.
